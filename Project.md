**Executive Summary**

_This analysis reviews historical sales data to identify key revenue drivers, profitability trends, top-performing products, and growth opportunities. The goal is to help the business understand where to focus resources in order to increase revenue and improve profitability._

Average Order Value (AOV) shows a declining trend over time, indicating that customers are spending less per transaction on average. Despite this, order volumes increased consistently, suggesting stronger customer acquisition or increased purchasing frequency.
This shift initially contributed to slower sales growth, as declining AOV offset rising demand. However, in later periods, total sales recovered, driven primarily by higher order volumes, which compensated for smaller basket sizes.
Overall, revenue performance is driven by both order volume and AOV. Monitoring these metrics together helps identify whether growth is being driven by more customers, higher spend per customer, or both.
Future revenue growth can be achieved by increasing order frequency and improving basket size through targeted upselling and cross-selling strategies.

Recommendations:
The business demonstrates strong growth, with profit increasing faster than sales, indicating improving operational efficiency. The focus should now shift toward sustaining this growth by scaling high-performing product categories and improving average transaction value.

3. Regional & City Profitability
The West Region is the most profitable, representing the strongest overall performance. Within this region, Los Angeles is the highest profit-contributing city, highlighting it as a key growth and profitability hub.
The Central Region is the least profitable, suggesting potential issues such as weaker demand, pricing pressure, higher operating costs, or inefficient distribution. This region requires further investigation to identify the main drivers of underperformance.
At the city level, New York City is the highest profit contributor overall (62.04K), making it a critical driver of business profitability.
In contrast, Philadelphia is a loss-making city, indicating structural issues such as high costs, low margins, or poor product-market fit. This city requires targeted intervention, particularly around pricing strategy, cost control, and sales effectiveness.

4. Strategic Recommendations
Focus Areas for Faster Growth:
1. Invest More in West Region
The West Region is the strongest performing region and should be used as a growth engine.
•	Expand marketing
•	Increase stock availability
•	Replicate success strategies in other regions
2. Fix Loss-Making Products
Unprofitable products are reducing overall margins and should be reviewed.
•	Review Cisco TelePresence pricing
•	Remove or renegotiate Eureka bag product
3. Strengthen High-Profit Cities
Focus on cities that already generate strong returns to maximise profitability.
•	Increase campaigns in New York and Los Angeles
4. Improve Central Region Performance
•	Investigate logistics, demand, and pricing issues
5. Increase Customer Retention
•	Implement loyalty programs
•	Encourage repeat purchases through targeted promotions and engagement campaigns

While both sales and profit have grown over time, profitability remains uneven across products and regions. This indicates that revenue growth alone is not sufficient; strategic focus must also be placed on margin optimisation and efficient resource allocation. Fluctuations in profit margins suggest pricing pressure and discounting effects in certain periods, reinforcing the need for disciplined revenue management.

Final Conclusion
The business has demonstrated strong long-term growth, with sales increasing by 51.4% and profit rising by 88.6% between 2014 and 2017. This indicates not only expanding revenue but also improving profitability over time.
Growth is primarily driven by high-performing regions such as the West, with key cities like New York and Los Angeles serving as major profit contributors. However, performance is uneven across the business, with certain products and regions negatively impacting overall profitability.
These underperforming areas highlight the need for targeted intervention in pricing, cost management, and regional strategy. Moving forward, sustained growth will depend on scaling high-performing segments while addressing structural inefficiencies in weaker areas.
